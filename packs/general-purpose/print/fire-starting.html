Fire starting — ferro rod, friction, lens

Three reliable ignition methods and the tinder structure all of them need.

General Purpose SIGNAL v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Gather before you ignite. Tinder, kindling, and fuel sorted into three piles within arm's reach. Most failures are a missing pile, not a missing spark.
  2. Build the lay. Tinder bundle the size of a tennis ball, surrounded by kindling the diameter of a pencil, sheltered from wind.
  3. Ignite low, feed slow. Spark into the tinder, breathe gently, add pencil-thick wood only after a sustained flame, finger-thick wood after that.

Detail

Ferro rod (most reliable)

  • Strip a thick spine from your knife back; the cutting edge dulls fast on the rod.
  • Hold the rod against the tinder, not above it. Pull the rod toward you so the tinder does not get knocked.
  • Birch bark, fatwood shavings, char cloth, dry grass, cotton ball with petroleum jelly all catch sparks reliably.

Solar lens

  • Magnifying lens, eyeglasses with water film, or the bottom of a polished can.
  • Focus to the smallest possible point on dark, dry tinder. Hold steady — sustained heat ignites; flicker does not.
  • Needs direct sun. Useless in overcast.

Friction (bow drill)

  • Spindle (15–20 cm, dry softwood), hearth board (dry cedar, willow, cottonwood), bow (curved branch + cordage), handhold (hard knot or stone with a depression).
  • Burn in a socket on the hearth, then carve a V-notch from the edge to the socket so dust falls onto a leaf.
  • Long, steady strokes until smoke is constant and a coal forms in the notch. Transfer coal to tinder bundle, blow into flame.
  • Requires practice. Do not attempt this as your only method if cold or wet.

Tinder structure

  • Fluff inner bark or grass between palms until it is hair-fine.
  • Form a bird's nest with a depression in the centre.
  • Drop the spark or coal in, fold the nest around it, breathe through it from below.

Do not

Do not light a fire on dry duff, peat, or under a snow-laden conifer canopy. Duff and peat smoulder underground for days and start wildfires after you leave. Snow loads on branches dump onto the fire as heat rises. Clear to mineral soil or rock in a 1 m radius before ignition.

Signal that you are out of scope

Hypothermia symptoms (uncontrolled shivering, slurred speech, fumbling) mean fine-motor fire starting will fail. Get into shelter and into dry insulation first; calorie intake and a shared body-warmth shelter are faster routes to warmth than a doubtful fire. Active wildfire conditions, fire bans, or proximity to fuel (gasoline, propane, dry grass within 3 m): do not ignite — find another way to warm or signal.
