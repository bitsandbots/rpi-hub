Eight knots that do everything useful

A short repertoire that covers loops, hitches, bends, and stoppers. Practise each ten times with eyes closed.

General Purpose rpi-hub v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Name the job. Loop, hitch, bend, or stopper. Each row of the table below answers one job.
  2. Pick the knot that is hardest to misremember. Bowline, clove, sheet bend, figure-eight cover 80 % of field needs.
  3. Dress and load-test. Pull each strand snug; load to body weight before trusting life-safety.

Detail

KnotFamilyUseNotes
BowlineLoopFixed loop at the end of a line — rescue, hauling."Rabbit out of the hole, around the tree, back down." Backs up well; can loosen unloaded.
Figure-eight on a bightLoopStrong fixed loop mid-line or end. Climbing tie-in.Easy to inspect. Strongest common knot.
Clove hitchHitchQuick tie-off to a post or carabiner — guy lines, tarp ridge.Slips under inconsistent load; back it up with a half-hitch.
Taut-line hitchHitchAdjustable tension on a guy line — tarps, tents.Slides only when not loaded; locks under tension.
Trucker's hitchCompoundMechanical advantage to cinch a load — vehicle cargo, ridgelines.Roughly 2:1 purchase; finish with two half-hitches.
Sheet bendBendJoins two ropes, even of different diameter.Double up (double sheet bend) for slippery line.
Double fisherman'sBendPermanently joins two cords — prusik loops, repair.Hard to untie after loading; that is the point.
Figure-eight stopperStopperPrevents line running through a hole or device.Bulkier and more visible than an overhand.

Do not

Do not use a granny knot or a square knot to join two ropes — both fail under shock or alternating load. Square knot is for binding (a package), not for joining lines. Use a sheet bend or double fisherman's instead.

Signal that you are out of scope

Life-safety rigging — rescue lowers, lead climbing, rope access — requires gear inspection, redundancy, and trained backup. A field card is not a substitute. Use these knots for utility (shelter, hauling, restraint of objects) and call for a trained rigger when the load is a human body.
