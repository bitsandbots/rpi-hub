Water purification — field methods

When the tap is out and you have water of unknown safety. Choose the method by what you have on hand.

General Purpose rpi-POD v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Pre-filter. Pour through a clean cloth or coffee filter into a clean container. Cloudy water defeats every method below.
  2. Pick one method. Boil if you have fuel. Chemical (chlorine or iodine) if you do not. UV or filter if you carry one.
  3. Wait the full time. Do not drink before the contact time on the table below has elapsed.

Do not

Do not skip the pre-filter or the contact time. Cloudy water shields pathogens from chlorine, iodine, and UV. Two extra minutes of straining and 30 minutes of waiting are the difference between safe water and a gastrointestinal illness you cannot treat in the field.

Signal that you are out of scope

Water from an industrial spill, agricultural runoff, oil sheen, or downstream of a chemical plant. Field methods address microbiological hazards only — they do not remove heavy metals, pesticides, fuel, or radiological contamination. If the source is suspect, source elsewhere or wait for a tested supply.
