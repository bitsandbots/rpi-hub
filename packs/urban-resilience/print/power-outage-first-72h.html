Power outage — first 72 hours at home

Sheltering in place when the grid is down. Focus on food, heat / cool, communication, and carbon-monoxide safety.

Urban Resilience rpi-POD v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Keep the fridge and freezer shut. Closed fridge holds safe temps about 4 hours, full freezer about 48 hours. Every open is hours lost.
  2. Decide on heating / cooling. In summer above 32 °C (90 °F) or winter below 4 °C (40 °F) indoors, identify a safer location (community cooling/heating centre, family, hotel) — especially for infants, elderly, or anyone with chronic illness.
  3. Plug nothing combustion into a closed space. Generators, grills, propane heaters, and camp stoves are outdoor-only.

Detail

First hour

  • Check whether the outage is just your unit (breaker), your building, or the neighbourhood. Look for streetlights and neighbour windows.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics — surges happen when power returns.
  • Leave one lamp switched on so you know when power returns.
  • Note time of outage; you will need it for food-safety decisions.

Food safety

  • Fridge ≤ 4 °C / 40 °F is safe; above for > 2 h, discard perishables (meat, eggs, dairy, cooked leftovers).
  • Freezer: if items still contain ice crystals or are ≤ 4 °C, they can be refrozen or cooked.
  • "When in doubt, throw it out" — food poisoning during a wider emergency is a hospital admission you cannot get.

Heat / cool

  • Summer: lowest floor, blinds drawn, cross-ventilate at night, hydrate aggressively, cool wrists / neck with damp cloths.
  • Winter: one small room, close interior doors, layer clothes, tent under blankets, share body heat. Eat calories.
  • Watch infants, elderly, and people on medications that impair temperature regulation. Check on neighbours.

Communication and light

  • Battery / hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts and rpi-POD hub for offline content.
  • LED lanterns and headlamps. No open flames near children, pets, or anything flammable.
  • One phone in airplane mode preserves battery for SMS, which works on weaker signal than voice.

Do not

Do not run a generator, charcoal grill, gasoline camp stove, or propane heater rated for outdoor use indoors, in a garage (even with the door open), or near a window, door, or vent. Carbon-monoxide is colourless and odourless; it kills sleeping people in minutes. Install or test battery-powered CO alarms at the start of any outage where combustion appliances are in use.

Signal that you are out of scope

Anyone in the household depends on powered medical equipment (oxygen concentrator, dialysis, refrigerated medication, electric wheelchair, CPAP for severe sleep apnoea) — that is a pre-event call to the utility's medical baseline list and a planned move to a powered location, not a 72-hour field decision. Symptoms of CO poisoning (headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion in multiple household members at once) — get everyone outside into fresh air and call emergency services.
Sources: FEMA / Ready.gov "Power Outages"; CDC "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning After a Disaster" and "Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency"; USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service "A Consumer's Guide to Food Safety"; Wikipedia "Power outage".
