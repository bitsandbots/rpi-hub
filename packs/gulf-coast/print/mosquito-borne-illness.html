Mosquito-borne illness — recognise + treat

Common febrile illnesses to suspect in Gulf-Coast and post-flood conditions. Prevention is the only effective field treatment.

Gulf Coast rpi-hub v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Prevent bites. EPA-registered repellent (DEET 20–30 %, picaridin 20 %, or IR3535) on exposed skin; permethrin-treated clothing; long sleeves; window screens; drain standing water within 100 m of shelter weekly.
  2. Recognise fever pattern. Fever within 2–14 days of bites in a high-risk area is mosquito-borne illness until proven otherwise.
  3. Hydrate and refer. Oral rehydration, paracetamol for fever. Avoid aspirin / NSAIDs until dengue is excluded. Seek clinical care for diagnosis.

Detail

IllnessTypical featuresWatch for
West Nile virus Most infected people have no symptoms. About 1 in 5 develop fever, headache, body aches, sometimes rash. Symptoms 2–14 days after bite. Severe headache, neck stiffness, confusion, weakness, seizures — neuroinvasive disease, urgent.
Dengue Sudden high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, joint and muscle pain ("breakbone"), rash. 4–10 days after bite. Days 3–7 are the danger window: warning signs are persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, bleeding gums or nose, lethargy, restlessness — severe dengue, urgent.
Zika Mild: low-grade fever, rash, conjunctivitis, joint pain. Many cases asymptomatic. Pregnancy — Zika causes congenital defects; pregnant people should avoid exposure and seek care if symptomatic.
Eastern equine encephalitis Rare in Gulf states but high case-fatality. Fever, chills, severe headache progressing to encephalitis. Altered mental status, seizures — immediate emergency care.
Malaria (imported / rare endemic) Cyclical fever, chills, sweats, headache, GI symptoms. Onset 7 days to several months after exposure. Recent travel to endemic regions makes malaria a must-rule-out — needs lab diagnosis and prescription treatment.

Do not

Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen to anyone with fever in an area with active dengue transmission until dengue is excluded by a clinician. These drugs raise bleeding risk in dengue and can convert a recoverable illness into a severe one. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) at standard doses is the safer antipyretic.

Signal that you are out of scope

Confusion, neck stiffness, seizures, persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, bleeding from gums or nose, rash with petechiae, fever above 39.5 °C (103 °F) for more than 48 hours, or any febrile illness in an infant under 3 months, a pregnant person, or someone immunocompromised. Field measures do not diagnose or treat mosquito-borne viral encephalitis or severe dengue — clinical care is required.
