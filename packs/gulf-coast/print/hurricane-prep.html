Hurricane prep — 72 hours before landfall

A three-day countdown for households in a NHC-forecast cone.

Gulf Coast SIGNAL v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Know your zone. If your address is in a mandatory-evacuation zone or a surge-inundation zone, plan to leave — well before the order.
  2. Stage essentials now. Water (1 gal / person / day × 7 days), shelf-stable food (7 days), medicine (30 days), cash, documents in a waterproof bag.
  3. Set the decision rule. If a Hurricane Warning is issued or fuel lines and gridlock appear, you have already left it too long.

Detail

Time before landfallAction
T-72 hConfirm zone; choose a destination (inland family, shelter, hotel); top up fuel; refill prescriptions; cash from ATM; back up phones; charge banks; check NOAA WX radio batteries.
T-48 hFill water containers; freeze plastic bottles for cold storage; clear loose yard objects (chairs, grills, planters); trim weak branches if safe; photograph rooms for insurance; review evacuation route + an alternate.
T-36 hBoard windows if local code allows; fuel generators (store fuel outside, not in living space); raise valuables above expected surge; pre-cook a day of food; fully charge devices.
T-24 hIf in evacuation zone: leave. Roads will deteriorate, fuel will run out, contraflow may be enabled — drive with a half tank or more at all times.
T-12 hIf sheltering in place: fill bathtubs (sanitation water, not drinking); turn fridge / freezer to coldest; identify interior room with no windows; close interior doors to compartmentalise.
T-0Stay inside. Stay away from windows. Eye-of-the-storm calm lasts minutes then reverses — do not go outside.

Do not

Do not run a portable generator, charcoal grill, or camp stove indoors, in a garage, or near windows. Carbon-monoxide poisoning is the leading post-hurricane killer. Generators must be outdoors, at least 6 m (20 ft) from any window, door, or vent, and never refuelled while running.

Signal that you are out of scope

You are in a mandatory-evacuation zone but cannot leave (no vehicle, no money for fuel, mobility limitation, dependent care). Call the local emergency-management non-emergency line before the storm — most counties run pickup lists and special-needs shelters that close 24 hours before landfall. Once a Hurricane Warning is in effect, road conditions, downed lines, and storm surge can make late evacuation more dangerous than sheltering in place; the call is then a sheltering decision, not an evacuation one.
Sources: NOAA National Hurricane Center public-preparedness materials; FEMA "Hurricane Preparedness"; CDC "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning After a Disaster"; Wikipedia "Tropical cyclone preparedness" and "Storm surge".
