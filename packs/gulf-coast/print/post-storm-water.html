Post-storm water safety

Tap, well, floodwater, and contact-with-skin guidance after a hurricane or major flood.

Gulf Coast rpi-hub v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Assume contaminated. Treat all tap, well, and standing water as unsafe until your utility issues an all-clear and you have flushed your lines.
  2. Drink only treated water. Bottled, or boiled for 1 minute, or chemically disinfected (see general-purpose water-purification card).
  3. Avoid skin contact with floodwater. If contact happens, wash with soap and clean water; cover any open wound with a waterproof dressing.

Detail

Tap water

  • Listen for a Boil-Water Notice from your utility — assume one is in effect after a major storm.
  • When the notice lifts, flush each tap cold for 5 minutes, then run hot for 15 minutes (drain hot-water heater first if power was off long enough to cool it).
  • Replace point-of-use filters; bacterial growth in stagnant filter media is common.

Private well

  • If floodwater reached the wellhead, presume contamination.
  • Shock-chlorinate per state extension service guidance, then test before drinking.
  • Do not pump a flooded well for at least 24 hours after waters recede; sediment can damage the pump.

Floodwater + standing water

  • Contains sewage, fuel, agricultural chemicals, fire-ant rafts, snakes, sharps, and downed-line current.
  • Wear rubber boots and gloves. Goggles if splashing.
  • Discard any food or stored water that touched floodwater — including sealed cans if seams were submerged.

Container storage

  • Store treated water in food-grade containers, cool and dark, replace every 6 months.
  • One gallon per person per day for drinking, cooking, and basic hygiene; double in heat.

Do not

Do not drive or wade through moving floodwater. Six inches knocks an adult off their feet; two feet floats a car. Do not assume a tap is safe just because it runs clear — chlorine residual is your indicator that disinfection is intact, and you cannot see it. When in doubt, boil.

Signal that you are out of scope

Watery diarrhoea lasting more than 24 hours, bloody diarrhoea at any duration, fever above 38.5 °C (101 °F), or signs of dehydration in children or elderly (dry mouth, no tears, lethargy, no urine in 8 hours). Vibrio wound infections in Gulf waters can progress within hours — any wound that was exposed and becomes red, warm, or painful out of proportion needs medical evaluation immediately, not first aid alone.
Sources: CDC "Drinking Water Advisories" and "Vibrio Illness"; EPA "Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water" and well-water guidance; FEMA flood-recovery materials; Wikipedia "Boil-water advisory" and "Floodwater".
