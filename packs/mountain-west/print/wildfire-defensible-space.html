Wildfire — defensible space + go-bag

What to prepare before fire season and what to grab when an evacuation notice arrives.

Mountain West rpi-hub v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Zone your property. Three concentric zones around any structure (0–5 ft, 5–30 ft, 30–100 ft) get progressively less aggressive vegetation management.
  2. Pack the go-bag now. Documents, medicine, phone chargers, N95 masks, water, snacks, clothes, sturdy shoes, pet supplies — by the door at "Set" stage.
  3. Leave early. At "Go" or any time you feel unsafe. Roads choke fast in mountain valleys.

Detail

Defensible space zones (NWCG / IBHS)

  • Zone 0 — 0 to 5 ft from structure (Immediate): non-combustible only. No mulch, no shrubs, no woodpile. Clean gutters and roof of needles. Metal mesh (1/8 in) over vents.
  • Zone 1 — 5 to 30 ft (Intermediate): well-spaced, well-watered, low-growth plants. Trim limbs 6–10 ft up. Remove ladder fuels under trees. Move woodpiles and propane to Zone 2.
  • Zone 2 — 30 to 100+ ft (Extended): thin trees so canopies do not touch. Remove dead/down fuel. Mow grass to 4 in.
  • Address visible from the road, hose connected to a hydrant or tank, driveway wide enough for fire apparatus.

Go-bag and evacuation

  • One bag per person, packed and accessible.
  • Documents in a waterproof pouch: ID, insurance, deeds, medication list.
  • Prescription medication for 7 days, glasses, hearing-aid batteries.
  • N95 or P100 mask per person for smoke; goggles.
  • Phone, chargers, battery bank, paper map of the area.
  • Two days of water and shelf-stable food.
  • Sturdy long-sleeve cotton clothing, leather gloves, closed-toe shoes — what you would wear, not pack.
  • Pet carrier, leash, food, vaccination records.
  • Ready — fire in the area; review plan. Set — bags in the car, animals contained, decide route. Go — leave immediately by the pre-planned route.

Do not

Do not wait to see flames before leaving. Wind-driven fire in dry timber moves faster than a vehicle on a winding mountain road. Embers travel kilometres ahead of the front and ignite homes from the inside out — through open windows, vents, and decks. If the area is under an evacuation order, the time to defend property has passed.

Signal that you are out of scope

Active fire on three sides, smoke that blocks driving visibility, or any sense that the chosen route is no longer passable. Shelter in place becomes the choice only when egress has failed: get inside a sound structure, close all doors and windows, remove curtains, fill sinks and tubs, dress in cotton long sleeves, breathe through a damp cloth, and move room to room as fire passes. Wildland firefighters are not first-aid responders — for burns over more than the area of one palm, airway involvement, or any inhalation injury, that is a medical evacuation, not a field decision.
