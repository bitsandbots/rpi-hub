Winter survival — shelter, signal, stay warm

Stranded in cold conditions in vehicle or backcountry. Priorities are shelter first, signal second, warmth throughout.

Mountain West rpi-hub v1.2 · field card

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Stop. Stay put. Travelling on foot in winter kills more lost people than the cold does — your vehicle or last-known location is the easiest object to find.
  2. Insulate from the ground. Conduction loss is faster than air loss. Sit on a pack, branches, floor mats, anything that breaks contact with snow or metal.
  3. Signal continuously. Bright cloth, mirror, fire, whistle, vehicle horn in patterns of three. Searchers find signals, not silent shelters.

Detail

Vehicle

  • Stay in the vehicle. Tie a bright cloth to the antenna or door.
  • Run the engine for 10 minutes per hour for heat. Clear snow from the exhaust pipe each cycle — carbon-monoxide kills silently.
  • Crack a downwind window slightly when running the engine.
  • Layer everything you have, including floor mats and seat covers. Conserve battery.
  • Move arms and legs to maintain circulation. Eat and drink — calories and water generate heat.

Backcountry

  • Get out of wind: leeward side of a rock or tree, snow trench, dug-out tree well.
  • Quinzhee / snow shelter: pile snow 1 m deep, let it set 1 h, hollow out with a ventilation hole.
  • Insulate from ground with boughs or pack. Lid the entrance with a pack once inside.
  • Body-heat will warm a small interior; ventilate enough to avoid CO2 buildup.

Layered clothing (WICK / WARM / WIND)

  • Wick: synthetic or wool base layer against skin. Cotton kills — it stops insulating when wet.
  • Warm: fleece or down mid-layer for loft.
  • Wind: shell against wind and precipitation.
  • Cover head, neck, hands, feet. Most heat is lost wherever circulation is closest to the surface.

Recognising cold injury

  • Hypothermia: shivering, fumbling, mumbling, stumbling. Severe: shivering stops, confusion, drowsiness — emergency.
  • Frostbite: white waxy skin, hard to the touch, numb. Do not rub. Rewarm in 37–39 °C (99–102 °F) water once you can keep it thawed.
  • Calories, hot fluids, dry insulation, shared body heat.

Do not

Do not drink alcohol — it dilates skin vessels and accelerates heat loss while masking the warning signs. Do not rewarm frostbite in front of a fire (uneven heating destroys tissue) and do not let it thaw if there is any chance of refreezing — refreezing tissue is worse than leaving it frozen until rescue.

Signal that you are out of scope

Severe hypothermia (shivering stopped, altered consciousness), frostbite with blisters or blackened tissue, exhaustion that prevents continued effort, or an avalanche burial without locator and probe. Field rewarming of severe hypothermia is risky — handle the patient gently (rough handling can trigger fatal arrhythmias), insulate completely, and signal for medical evacuation.
Sources: NOAA NWS "Winter Storm Safety"; CDC "Extreme Cold Prevention Guide"; Wilderness Medical Society guidance summarised in Wikipedia "Hypothermia" and "Frostbite"; Ready.gov winter weather pages.
