Cascadia shake — first 30 minutes

A great-subduction-zone event will last 3–5 minutes with M9 potential. Plan in advance; act now.

Pacific Northwest SIGNAL v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. During shaking. Drop, Cover, Hold On. Get under a sturdy table; if none, against an interior wall away from windows and tall furniture.
  2. Shaking stops. Count to 60. Aftershocks follow. Check yourself and the people next to you for bleeding before standing.
  3. Within 10 minutes. If you are within sight of the coast or a tsunami inundation sign, walk inland or uphill now. Do not wait for an official warning — the wave can arrive in 15–30 minutes.

Detail

Indoors

  • Stay where you are. Most injuries are from moving during shaking.
  • Protect head and neck. If in bed, stay there and cover your head with a pillow.
  • After shaking, exit only if the building is visibly damaged or you smell gas. Use stairs, never elevators.
  • Shut off gas at the meter only if you smell or hear leaking. Once off, only a utility can safely restore it.

Outdoors / vehicle

  • Outdoors: move to open ground away from buildings, power lines, and trees. Drop and stay down.
  • Driving: pull over clear of overpasses, bridges, and power lines. Set the brake, stay in the vehicle, wait it out.
  • On the coast: any natural sign — strong shaking, ground roll for 20 seconds, sudden withdrawal of the sea — is your tsunami warning. Move uphill / inland on foot. Do not drive if roads are choked.

Do not

Do not run outside during shaking, do not stand in a doorway (modern doorways are no stronger than the rest of the wall), and do not wait for cell service or an official tsunami alert before evacuating low coastal ground. The infrastructure that delivers official alerts will be the first thing damaged.

Signal that you are out of scope

Trapped survivors, structural collapse, suspected spinal injury, sustained chest pain, or burns over more than the area of one palm. Mark the location, do not move the patient unless the immediate environment is more dangerous than the move, and signal for trained search-and-rescue. If you smell gas and cannot reach the meter, evacuate the building and warn neighbours rather than entering further.
Sources: USGS Cascadia Subduction Zone publications; FEMA "Earthquake Safety at Home"; NOAA NWS Tsunami Program; Washington / Oregon Emergency Management public guidance; Wikipedia "Cascadia subduction zone".
