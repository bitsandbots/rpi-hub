Tsunami — high-ground heuristics

Coastal evacuation when an official warning may not arrive before the wave.

Pacific Northwest rpi-POD v1.2 · field card
Review before field use. Field cards are condensed references, not certified curricula. Verify against your local protocols and have a qualified responder review before relying on them in an emergency.

Decision in 30 seconds

  1. Recognise the trigger. Any of: shaking that lasts 20 seconds or longer; sudden withdrawal of the sea exposing rocks or fish; a loud roar from offshore; an official Tsunami Warning.
  2. Move on foot. Inland and uphill. Aim for 30 m (100 ft) elevation or 3 km (2 mi) inland — whichever you reach first.
  3. Stay high for hours. The first wave is rarely the largest. Wait for an all-clear from authorities, not the silence after the first surge.

Detail

Local (near-field) tsunami

  • Generated by a Cascadia rupture under your feet — arrival 15 to 30 minutes after shaking stops.
  • No official warning may reach you in time. Treat the shake itself as the warning.
  • Walk, do not drive — roads will be cracked, jammed, and blocked by downed lines.
  • Designated tsunami evacuation routes (blue arrow signs) point to mapped high ground.

Distant (far-field) tsunami

  • Generated by a distant earthquake — arrival hours later, with official Watch / Advisory / Warning.
  • Follow NOAA / NWS messaging. Tune rpi-POD Listen to your local NOAA frequency.
  • Watch → no action yet, prepare. Advisory → stay off beaches and marinas. Warning → evacuate low ground.

What to carry if you have 60 seconds

  • Shoes, jacket, water, phone, medicine, ID. That is the entire list.
  • If you have a vertical-evacuation building (concrete, 4+ stories) nearby, that is an option when uphill is too far.

Do not

Do not go down to the beach to watch the sea withdraw — that is the trough preceding the wave, and the wave runs faster than you can. Do not return to low ground after the first wave; subsequent waves can be larger and arrive over several hours.

Signal that you are out of scope

You cannot reach mapped high ground on foot in 15 minutes from a beach, harbour, or low-lying coastal road. That is a planning failure, not a field decision — identify a vertical evacuation refuge (a strong reinforced-concrete building of four stories or more, or a purpose-built tsunami tower) in advance. After the event, treat all standing water as contaminated and infrastructure as failed until inspected.
Sources: NOAA National Tsunami Warning Center; USGS tsunami research publications; FEMA P-646 "Guidelines for Design of Structures for Vertical Evacuation from Tsunamis"; Wikipedia "Tsunami warning system" and "Cascadia subduction zone".
